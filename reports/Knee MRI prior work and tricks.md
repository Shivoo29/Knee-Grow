# Retrain one strong model, then merge

The quickest route from 0.943 toward top 10 is to stop tuning the public stack and rebuild our own single model to the level of the public frontier. Competitors report single CoAtNet, ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt models at **0.949–0.955 public LB**, and they got there with recipe choices we can copy: all 4,349 report-labelled studies, an average of several soft-label tables, a 140–154 mm field of view, 80–96 slices, dense inference windows and top-3 epoch averaging. After that, the next steps are a teacher→student round that mixes out-of-fold (OOF) predictions into the labels, aimed at our weak findings (Lateral OA, PF OA, Synovitis), and a merge with a team that has an independent model of 0.950 or better before the **Oct 15** deadline. Our own model scores **0.8925 on gold-58**. Public models at roughly **0.930 gold reach 0.949–0.950 LB** ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)), so most of the gap to the leaders sits in our own model and not in blending. Note the target: **0.961 is currently a tie for ranks 8–13, and 0.962 sits at ranks 5–7** ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard)), so a safe top-10 needs about 0.962 on private. The literature and earlier RSNA winners agree with this direction: 2.5D slice encoders with attention pooling are the right family, OOF label cleaning gave about +1% in LSDC 2024, and ROI or localisation gains are large but cost too much for a two-week window. External data (OAI) is the one lever with a measured in-competition gain on exactly our weakest three findings, but its rule status is unresolved. It is a team decision, not a default.

## Prior work ranks by similarity to our setup

The closest analogue is this competition's own public material, followed by the RSNA 2024 lumbar-spine challenge (LSDC). The academic knee-MRI literature is mostly useful for setting ceilings. The table scores each body of work on how closely its data, labels and constraints match ours.

| Prior work | Similarity | Why | Most transferable lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 RSNA Knee public notebooks and threads | Identical | Same data, metric and 9 h 2×T4 limit | Own retrained single model ≥0.95 beats a 25-model public stack ([746792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792)) |
| RSNA LSDC 2024 | High | Multi-series MRI (sag/cor/ax, T1 + fluid-sensitive), many conditions per study, same Kaggle time limit | Attention-MIL + BiLSTM + aux attention loss; OOF noise cleaning +1% ([1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/540091), [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/ianpan-kevin-yuji-bartley-2nd-place-solution)) |
| Eur Radiol 2025 multi-condition knee MRI | High (task), medium (scale) | 3,121 routine studies, report-derived labels, shared slice encoder + transformer | Our family is right; report labels cap subjective findings ([PMC13035746](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13035746)) |
| RSNA Aneurysm 2025, Trauma 2023, C-spine 2022 | Medium | 3D volumes, 2.5D CNN + sequence/attention, 9 h limit | ROI crops and dense auxiliary losses give the biggest wins but need localisation labels ([Aneurysm 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)) |
| RANZCR 2021, COVID 2021 | Medium (macro-AUC, noisy labels) | 2D X-ray, but macro AUC with tight score spreads | Simple averages hold up on private; soft pseudo-labels beat hard ([RANZCR 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ranzcr-clip-catheter-line-classification/discussion/226633)) |
| CheXpert/VisualCheXbert report-label studies | Medium (labels) | Report-derived labels, evaluation on image-read labels | Labels help only when they move toward image truth ([Jain 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.00793)) |
| MRNet, ELNet, Tack (OAI) | Low–medium | Single-site, 1–3 tasks, small validation sets | Pretraining and plane fusion matter; localisation helps the weakest subregions most ([Tack](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8675251)) |

Two calibration points from the literature matter for us. In the one controlled study, a labeller that was better on report-level F1 (CheXbert) gave **about zero image-AUC gain**. An image-grounded labeller (VisualCheXbert) gave **+0.04 mean AUROC**, with +0.14 to +0.16 on hedge-prone findings ([Jain 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.00793)). Gold-58 was reportedly annotated from images, blind to the reports ([745861](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745861), an unverified participant claim). If that is true, moving labels toward image truth with OOF predictions should pay off more than prompt engineering. Second, published routine-protocol ceilings put **lateral meniscus at 0.77–0.84 and compartment cartilage at 0.74–0.85** ([Eur Radiol 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13035746)). Our Lat Men at .837 is already at that ceiling. Lat OA (.766), PF OA (.798) and MCL (.859, against a published MCL-tear 0.88–0.95) are where real headroom exists. An architkonde CoAtNet that reached 0.950 LB still had Synovitis .797, Lat OA .816 and PF OA .874 on gold ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)). The leaders are also weak on these findings, just less weak than we are.

## Single retrained models now beat the 25-model public stack

This competition's history is the strongest evidence available. The public "fork wall" climbed from 0.937 to 0.943 through blend-constant changes on a stack of 20 DINOv2 members, 5 A5 folds, RadImageNet heads, 4 Raptor views and 4 CoAt readers. That stack consumes **6.5–8 h of the 9 h limit** ([goodpjw2008](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-stack-2-5d-convnext-mil-lb-0-944)). In that time the top of the board was built on strong single models. Reported single-model scores include **miyamotodaiya 0.955 (5-fold), coolinlai 0.954 (5-fold ResNet-50 at 224), andy2709 0.950 (Qwen3.5-2B VLM LoRA), architkonde 0.950 (single-fold 2.5D CoAtNet at 224, gold OOF 0.930) and laymond 0.949 (CoAtNet at 288)** ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)). On the efficiency leaderboard, **53 entries score ≥0.955 public**, including fast submissions at 0.959–0.962 ([efficiency LB](https://www.kaggle.com/code/ryanholbrook/rsna-knee-abnormalities-efficiency-lb)).

nartaa's published ladder is the most useful ablation for us, because it is a CoAtNet like ours and each step is a public LB number ([746792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792)). The steps were: all-data CoAtNet, 80 slices, 78 windows **0.939**; 96 slices, native 384 cache, 94 windows **0.940**; adding JEV labels to the blend **0.942**; adding 2,399 OAI knees with masked labels **0.945**; the 384 full-field external-data recipe **0.948**; anatomical-mirror TTA **0.949**. The training recipe was 16 epochs, backbone LR 4.01e-5, head LR 1.95e-3, wd 0.0588, 12 windows per study in training, batch 1 with 16-study gradient accumulation, and averaging epochs 14, 11 and 12. Dread Development's numbers add a corpus-size effect. Growing the training set from 3,155 to 4,349 studies raised gold from **0.8923 to 0.9054**, with the largest gains on Lat Men (+0.071), Fracture (+0.057) and Lat OA (+0.048). Going from 42 to 62 windows gave **+0.003** LB ([Dread notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/dreaddevelopment/knee-mri-twelve-findings-from-a-single-model); [737696](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/737696)). Our 0.8925 gold matches Dread's *pre-expansion* number almost exactly. That is a reason to check first that we train on all 4,349 studies and not a filtered subset.

On geometry, cody11null found that moving from a 100 mm to a 140 mm field of view "helped a ton", and 160 mm was similar. goodpjw uses 154 mm at 0.4 mm/px ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)). Some failures are silent and catastrophic, and they matter as much as any trick. Sorting slices by filename instead of ImagePositionPatient cost **0.0276 on one fold** ([745759](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745759)). Emulated bf16 on T4 turned a 0.851 model into 0.710 through time-guard fallbacks ([744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230)). A naive all-plane width flip TTA *hurt* (0.940→0.939), while an anatomically correct mirror helped ([746792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792)).

Weak members do damage. Blending a 0.860 reader at 0.2 into a 0.944 stack dropped it to **0.937** ([aditbytes](https://github.com/aditbytes/kaggle-rsna-knee-abnormality)). Members with holdout 0.85–0.87 at 15% weight dropped a 0.941 stack to 0.936 ([starkhushi](https://www.kaggle.com/code/starkhushi/rsna-knee-why-public-forks-stop-at-0-941)). Even a solid independent 0.929 ConvNeXt reader added only +0.001 to the 0.943 stack ([goodpjw2008](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-stack-2-5d-convnext-mil-lb-0-944)). Our current own model adds value only once it is at or above the level of the thing it is blended with.

The sequencing follows from the deadlines. **Oct 8–9:** the audit (#0), building the consensus label table (#2), and starting the frontier-recipe run (#1) on the L4 and a ResNet-50/ConvNeXt run (#5) on the 4070S. In parallel, open merge conversations. Candidates who have advertised include miyamotodaiya (0.955 single, seeking partners at 0.950+), kmatsu01 (0.940 single CoAtNet) and kishormorol (an independent DINOv2 arm) ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304); [744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230)). **Oct 11–14:** start 5-fold teachers for #4 while the first retrained model is scored on the LB. That LB number is our bargaining chip in merge talks, because a 0.950 partner will want to see an independent ≥0.945 model and not a public fork. **Oct 15:** merge decision. **Oct 16–21:** student retrain, fold ensemble, final blend with flat or global weights.

OAI (#8) is the hard call. It is the only lever with a measured in-competition gain that lands directly on Lat OA, PF OA and Synovitis. On Sep 21 the host said OAI is fine if it is "generally accessible… without an escalated (i.e. institutional) review", and senior competitors read that both ways ([741819](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/741819)). In a previous Kaggle competition (DeepFake Detection) the top two teams were removed for disallowed external data ([743416](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743416)). Forking nartaa's OAI-trained checkpoint inherits the same risk. Because our goal is top-10 and possibly a prize, the expected-value-maximising move is to keep OAI out of the main line and include it only in a second final submission, if the host has given an explicit answer by about Oct 14.

## Selection discipline protects the private score

Gold-58 cannot distinguish the changes this plan makes. With 5–10 positives a finding's AUC standard error is **0.07–0.11** at AUC 0.85–0.90, and macro-AUC SE is about **0.014–0.026** ([Hanley–McNeil](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiology.143.1.7063747), computed in the notes). In this competition gold has already misled people: caoyupeng saw **gold 0.945 but LB 0.910** for one label version, and Dread's +0.010 gold blend gain was "gold-set noise" ([745214](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745214); [Dread notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/dreaddevelopment/knee-mri-twelve-findings-from-a-single-model)). The haideptry fork that grid-searches per-target weights on gold, targeting "0.952–0.955+", is the pattern to avoid ([haideptry](https://www.kaggle.com/code/haideptry/rsna-knee-v2-vs-apex-comparative-study)). Past competitions say the same. Weights tuned on public LB cost VinBig's winners 0.007 private ([VinBig 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/vinbigdata-chest-xray-abnormalities-detection/discussion/231511)). The aneurysm team that picked epochs by public LB fell from 2nd to 5th (−0.046) ([5th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/5th-place-solution)). Simple averages won RANZCR 1st, LSDC 3rd and COVID 3rd ([RANZCR 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ranzcr-clip-catheter-line-classification/discussion/226633)).

In practice: select models by 5-fold OOF on all ~4,400 studies against the consensus labels, and use gold only as a paired-bootstrap sanity check. Never validate against labels you repaired, since "your CV will improve by definition" ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)). Blend by rank or logit average with at most a few global weights, and treat public moves of ±0.001–0.002 as noise. Make one final submission the best-LB flat-weight blend and the other the most OOF-robust blend, with no per-label routing tuned on public ([starkhushi](https://www.kaggle.com/code/starkhushi/rsna-knee-why-public-forks-stop-at-0-941)). In RANZCR, the AUC competition closest to ours in metric, the median top-50 rank change on private was 14 places ([RANZCR LB](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ranzcr-clip-catheter-line-classification/leaderboard)). A public 0.961 is therefore not a safe top-10. Robustness is worth a few thousandths of public score.

## Conclusion

Prior work changes where the effort should go. The 0.943→0.961 gap is a model-quality gap, not an ensembling gap. The public frontier went from a 25-model stack to one CoAtNet in two weeks, and the reported 0.950–0.955 models are plain 2.5D CNNs trained well on averaged soft labels. Our 0.8925 gold model is roughly where Dread's CoAtNet was before it trained on the full corpus. For that reason the first day should go to checking data coverage, slice ordering and label averaging, before any new architecture. Prior work also says our three weakest findings (Lat OA, PF OA, Synovitis) are the ones report labels describe worst. The two levers aimed at that are OOF label mixing, which is safe, and OAI masked labels, which carry rule risk. Both target image truth rather than better text parsing, which is what the CheXpert evidence predicts will work.

There is a real uncertainty. No team above 0.955 has published its recipe, and no cited source shows any combination of these steps reaching 0.961. A realistic solo outcome by Oct 22 is about **0.950–0.955 public**. Reaching 0.961–0.962 most likely needs a merge with an independent ≥0.950 model, which makes the Oct 15 deadline the binding constraint of the plan. Merge talks should start in parallel with the retrain, not after it.
