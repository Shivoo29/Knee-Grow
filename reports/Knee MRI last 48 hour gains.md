# Escape the 0.958 tie by Thursday

No technique still open to us can add +0.008 to +0.009 by the deadline (Thu 22 Oct, 23:59 UTC) without new external data. Lifting our 0.958 blend to the 0.967 top-ten line would take an own model whose errors overlap the public checkpoints' far less than ours do, a within-class correlation of about 0.67 where the public members sit at 0.86–0.89, and our two blends of earlier ConvNeXt-T models into the public recipe displayed **0.956 and 0.957, below the recipe's own 0.958**. The three teams that jumped since 16 Oct, two by +0.006 and one by +0.009, used private model families and published nothing, while every author of a public checkpoint but one is stuck at 0.958. The smaller gains matter more than their size suggests: **1,338 teams share 0.958**, the **silver (rank 312) and bronze (rank 625) lines both fall inside that block**, and we sit at **rank 672**, 47 places outside bronze. Most of the block appears to run a handful of public notebooks, so on the private board those copies will still land close together, and a real gain of a few ten-thousandths over the recipe lifts a team above most of them while a real loss drops it below. The plan follows from that: put the improved own model (round-2 labels, all-data seeds 310–315 and the adopted fixes) on the leaderboard today at a rank weight of 0.1–0.2 set by a quick correlation check, since it is the only lever that can display 0.959; test series TTA offline on the alternate-series corpus already built (510 of 1,146 studies) and ship it only if it wins there; add free refinements such as a mirror pass for the 224-px CoAtNet; and select two finals that bracket the risk, the pure public recipe as a floor and the recipe plus our model as the upside, because Kaggle ranks a team by the better of its two selected submissions. The realistic outcome is a public 0.958–0.959 and a medal that is plausible but not secured; gold and the top ten are out of reach.

The new checkpoints add nothing either: the R6 fine-tunes ("B-leg") are warm-started from R6, so they are correlated with it by construction, and their own team stayed at 0.958, while the Mycelis four-fold set's own logs show its gold-58 macro AUC falling to about 0.58 ([B-leg](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kritikabenjwal/rsna-knee-bleg-s0-f01); [Mycelis](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/neronrx/mycelis-rsna-knee-4fold-hive)). The one exception among public-component authors is bliverrigpdoge, who reached **0.959 by blending privately trained knee-centred CNNs into the public stack at weight 0.2**, the only public precedent for what we are attempting ([trainkit](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/bliverrigpdoge/rsna-knee-trainkit); [R7 + Raptor + knee-centred ConvNeXt](https://www.kaggle.com/code/bliverrigpdoge/r7-raptor-knee-centred-convnext)).

The teams that moved used their own models. In four days Hyunwoong Kim and BarryZhou each gained **+0.006** (0.961 → 0.967) and Leslie Yeh **+0.009** (0.954 → 0.963), none with public knee code ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard)), and the leading team's fast efficiency entry already scores 0.965 ([efficiency LB](https://www.kaggle.com/code/ryanholbrook/rsna-knee-abnormalities-efficiency-lb)). Leslie Yeh's jump shows that +0.009 in four days is possible, but it started from a lower base, with a private pipeline already running and 16 submissions to tune it. The only method hint from near the top in this window is colum2131's (0.962 → 0.964): a fixed-ratio blend of image pseudo-labels and LLM labels as training targets ([735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)), a recipe our round-2 labels already implement.

Arithmetic settles the question for us. A gain of +0.009 in macro AUC means +0.108 of summed AUC across the 12 findings, for example +0.027 on each of the four weak ones, which is the size of gain the literature reports from training on region crops, not from inference changes. Under a binormal model with optimal weights ([sensitivity index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_index)), our own model, at 0.948 alone, would need a within-class correlation with the 0.958 blend of **about 0.67 or less** to lift it to 0.967, and **about 0.89 or less** just to reach 0.959 (our computation). The public checkpoints correlate with each other at about 0.86–0.89 ([0.957 Explained](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/rsna-knee-0-957-explained-why-the-open-ensembl)), our distilled students sat near 0.925, and our blends of earlier ConvNeXt-T members displayed 0.956 at weight 0.3 (17 Oct) and 0.957 at weight 0.2. One public submission carries a noise SD of about 0.003 on roughly 390 studies ([748011](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748011)), so each of those blend results is weak evidence on its own, but they point the same way, and the gold line at 0.964 is out of reach for the same reason.

## Past RSNA winners averaged spare series, flipped, and kept ensembles small

Duplicate series were common in the 2024 lumbar-spine and 2023 abdominal-trauma contests, and the winners handled them simply.

| Contest and place | How duplicate series were handled |
|---|---|
| Lumbar spine, 2nd (Ian Pan's part) | Took crops from both duplicated axial series ([LSDC 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539452)) |
| Lumbar spine, 11th | Ran inference once per axial series and took "a simple average" ([LSDC 11th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539569)) |
| Lumbar spine, 6th | Merged several axial series into one stack sorted by position ([LSDC 6th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/541813)) |
| Lumbar spine, 14th | Refused to mix orientation groups in one stack ([LSDC 14th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539459)) |
| Lumbar spine, 7th | Kept only the highest-confidence axial series and called that choice "not necessarily the best" ([LSDC 7th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539486)) |
| Abdominal trauma, 7th and 8th | Averaged predictions across a patient's two series ([trauma 7th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447549); [trauma 8th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447706)) |
| Abdominal trauma, 1st | Took the maximum over every slice of both series, inside a pipeline built on max aggregation ([trauma 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447449)) |
| Abdominal trauma, 9th | Picked one series per target by contrast phase ([trauma 9th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447506)) |

**No write-up isolates the gain from averaging over same-type series**; the nearest figure is the lumbar 2nd place's 27-crop slice-and-offset aggregation, which "improved performance significantly" but came without a number. The plain mean is therefore the field-tested default, and the trauma 9th place's contrast-based pick is the precedent for our adopted rule that the axial slot prefers the fat-suppressed series.

Few inference-time gains were measured at all. Flip TTA gave the aneurysm 2nd place **+0.012 private AUC** at 4×, left/right label-swap augmentation with 8× TTA gave **+0.005** more, and a two-checkpoint ensemble added another +0.005 ([aneurysm 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/2nd-place-solution)). Small ensembles of genuinely different pipelines also paid: two models gave +0.019 private in aneurysm 15th ([aneurysm 15th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/15th-place-solution)), and the 18-model rank average that won SIIM-ISIC 2020, built without public-leaderboard feedback, gained +0.008 private over its best single model ([SIIM-ISIC 1st](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05351)). The failures teach as much. Every documented RSNA post-processing gain, from temperature to coefficient scaling and square-root rescaling, was a log-loss calibration, and a monotone transform of one finding's scores cannot change its AUC. Stacking overfit in trauma 3rd ([trauma 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447464)) and in aneurysm 9th, whose Nelder-Mead stack scored CV 0.858 against private 0.823 ([aneurysm 9th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/9th-place-solution)). The trauma 2nd place's best private score came from its strongest single model ([trauma 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447453)), and the aneurysm 18th place found that a dedicated T2 model added nothing and that false-positive filtering hurt AUROC ([aneurysm 18th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/18th-place-solution)).

Two further lessons bear on the final days. Full-data retrains went in as added members with a fixed recipe: the trauma 1st place's final ensemble mixed four-fold models with full-data models trained under several seeds ([trauma 1st repository](https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution)), the pattern of our all-data seeds 310–315, whereas the aneurysm 5th place shipped a single near-full-data model whose epoch was picked on the public leaderboard and fell from 2nd public to 5th private ([aneurysm 5th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/5th-place-solution)). Final picks made on one number also failed: the lumbar 13th place's CV-best submission, the one with the most TTA, was its worst on private, and its public-best ranked only third on private among its own submissions ([LSDC 13th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539510)).

### Series TTA can be judged on 1,146 studies before it ships

Series TTA for our model is the direct analogue of the lumbar 11th and trauma 7th and 8th means, and the corpus to test it is built: of the 1,146 fold-0 and gold studies, **510 (45%) have at least one alternate series for a slot**.

| Slot | Studies with an alternate series |
|---|---|
| Sagittal fat-sat | 134 |
| Sagittal plain | 161 |
| Coronal fat-sat | 95 |
| Coronal plain | 152 |
| **Axial** | **280** |

The test should run the fold-0 round-2 model on each alternate fill, average the per-finding probabilities with the first-pick pass, and compare first pick, mean and max on all 1,146 studies rather than only the 510, because averaging changes how multi-series studies rank against single-series ones; max should lose, since it pushes scores up for studies with more series. Two details decide whether the result helps. The axial alternates must stay inside the fat-suppressed group: our own check gave about 10% of fold-0 studies (97) a fat-suppressed axial instead of a plain one and raised PF OA, Lateral OA and Effusion by +0.014 to +0.015 on those studies, and averaging a plain axial back in would undo part of that gain. And the verdict should come from a paired bootstrap over the 1,088 out-of-fold studies, with gold-58 only as a harm check. The same precedent sets expectations: that fix touched 10% of studies and moved overall AUC by only about +0.001 per finding, while series TTA touches 45% of studies but averages rather than replaces, so **+0.0005 to +0.002 macro for our model** is a fair estimate, and a fifth or less of it survives a 0.2-weight blend (our estimates). The two other TTA options are smaller still. Our round-2 runs already evaluate 380 tiles, every window position of the 192-slice layout at both scales, so slice-offset TTA has nothing to add, and flip TTA on our model measured only **+0.0004 macro** on the 1,088 fold-0 studies after it had already been dropped for costing 1.5 times the inference time.

## The four weak findings sit near the published ceiling

MOAKS, the semi-quantitative knee OA scoring system, ties each weak finding to particular planes and sequences, and its reliability data show why their labels are noisy ([MOAKS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4058435)). Meniscal horns are scored on sagittal images and the body and extrusion on coronal; Hoffa-synovitis on mid-sagittal fat-suppressed images; effusion-synovitis on axial fluid-sensitive images; patellar cartilage on axial images; cartilage and marrow lesions on fat-suppressed spin-echo sequences; and osteophytes on non-fat-suppressed short-TE images. Inter-reader agreement is lowest exactly where we are weakest, at **κ 0.36 for tibial cartilage area** and 0.49 for tibial osteophytes, against **0.85 for patellar cartilage depth** and 0.95 for lateral meniscal morphology, and Hoffa-synovitis has an intra-rater κ of only 0.42.

The lateral meniscus is hard for readers and networks alike. A meta-analysis of 75 studies puts radiologists' sensitivity at 78.5% for lateral tears against 91.0% for medial ([meta-analysis summary](https://reachmd.com/news/mri-accuracy-for-knee-meniscus-tears-updated-meta-analysis/2487402/)). For lateral root tears the best single sign reaches 62% sensitivity, while a coronal cleft sign or a sagittal truncated triangle together reach **79% at 95% specificity** ([Yoshihara et al. 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12205407/)). A meniscus-cropped network scored 0.781 AUC on lateral tears against about 0.83 for two musculoskeletal radiologists, and **8 of its 10 lateral false negatives were also missed by at least one reader** ([Fritz et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7299917/)). Our 0.87–0.89 on gold already exceeds every routine-MRI result found, including 0.84 internal and 0.77 external for a 2025 multi-condition model ([Eur Radiol 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13035746)), though the reference standards differ; what remains sits in posterior-horn and root tears that need both planes, and in the labels.

Lateral OA is first a label problem and PF OA an anatomy problem. On gold-58, heliosli's knee-anchored R6 beats the CoAtNet half on Lateral OA by **0.865 to 0.776**, and heliosli's leaderboard-chosen weights give R6 85% of that vote ([goodpjw2008](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-does-s6-help-next-to-r6-lb-0-957); [heliosli V2](https://www.kaggle.com/code/heliosli/this-is-best-public-dataset-new-version-0-9586)), while darcywjoseph found every report extractor making the same five Lateral OA false positives on gold ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md)). Published networks reach 0.85 internally and 0.74 externally on tibial cartilage, one of the two largest external drops in that study ([Eur Radiol 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13035746)), so our ~0.81 sits inside the published band. For PF OA, severe trochlear lesions reach only 40–61% sensitivity on axial images, and the authors recommend reading every plane, sagittal included ([LaPrade et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6178377)); darcywjoseph's PF compartment crops moved PF OA by +0.000 ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md)). Our axial fat-sat preference is the evidence-backed fix, and given the patellar κ of 0.85, PF labels should be the most reliable of the four.

Synovitis without contrast has no deep-learning benchmark at all. Against contrast-enhanced synovitis, a non-contrast Hoffa grade of 1 or more has 71–88% sensitivity but only **30–55% specificity** ([Crema et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3578385)), and an OAI effusion classifier reached 61.5% accuracy ([Raman et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8873489/)). The leaderboard favours the CoAtNet half on synovitis, which gets 85% of the vote in heliosli's V2, even though gold-58 favours R6 by 0.859 to 0.812 ([goodpjw2008](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-does-s6-help-next-to-r6-lb-0-957)). Either gold-58's 27 positives mislead, or the CoAtNet's OAI supervision, which nartaa limited to PF OA, Lateral OA and Hoffa-synovitis ([nartaa RUNS.md](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/nartaa/rsna-knee-s6fall-weights)), transfers to the expert test labels. Either way, synovitis is where a model without OAI data is least likely to beat the public half.

| Finding | Our gold AUC | Where the signal lives | Public evidence (gold-58) | Still doable by Thursday |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lateral meniscus | 0.87–0.89 | Horns and root signs on sagittal fat-sat; body, cleft sign and extrusion on coronal fat-sat | R6 0.916, CoAtNet 0.894 (23 positives) | Series TTA through the 134 sagittal and 95 coronal fat-sat alternates |
| Lateral OA | ~0.81 | Cartilage and marrow on coronal fat-sat; osteophytes on plain series; κ 0.36 | R6 0.865, CoAtNet 0.776 (11 positives); leaderboard gives R6 85% | Keep the base weights; keep the scanner prior out of the floor final |

None of this can be settled on gold-58 by Thursday. With 11 to 27 positives per weak finding, one finding's AUC carries a standard error of roughly 0.04–0.07 ([Hanley & McNeil 1982](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiology.143.1.7063747)), and weights tuned on such gaps lost 0.001–0.003 on the leaderboard ([goodpjw2008](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-does-s6-help-next-to-r6-lb-0-957)). The literature's real lever for these findings, models trained on region crops, gained +0.04 to +0.07 on lateral-meniscus horns ([Tack et al. 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8675251)), but it needs training, and crops applied only at inference would be out of distribution. Three narrower conclusions hold: series TTA can reach the weak findings only through the fat-sat slots and the axial slot; the public base's per-finding weights should stay as they are; and our model should keep one global weight, since our own out-of-fold test of down-weighting it on Lateral OA was worth +0.0002 macro.

|---|---|---|
| 0.80 | 0.36 | 0.962 |

## Sixty-two hours: four submissions and a bracketed final pair

The schedule is set by Kaggle, not by the GPUs. Submissions waited 4–6 hours in the queue on 17 Oct ([750318](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/750318)) and our blend then runs for several hours, so plan on about 12 hours from submission to score. The weekly GPU quota resets on 24 Oct, after the deadline, so every commit run spends hours that will not come back ([750790](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/750790)); at most two GPU batch sessions run per account, and a CLI push without `--timeout` reserves 12 hours ([750318](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/750318)). Our 17 Oct commit, pinned to one T4, ran at 8.45 s per study against 4.35 s on two, so every commit log should show the dual-T4 line. Projected runtime should stay at or below 7.5 hours with the 8.4-hour kill guard as a backstop, and each alternate-series pass needs a per-study fallback to the first-pick prediction so that one malformed series can never fail the run ([749623](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/749623); [744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230)). No training job should start after seed 315, because there is no time left to validate one and score it. Two approvals unlock today's work: the L4 inference job for series TTA and the timed commit run.

Four submissions fit the window. Submission A is the public base with the 224-px mirror pass; if our base already scores that model with its mirror, skip A and let the existing 0.958 submission serve as both floor and control. Submission B adds our model to A at the checked weight, Submission C adds series TTA to B, and Submission D follows a rule fixed before any score arrives.

The rule for D is simple. It repeats the best configuration so far, C if series TTA passed and B otherwise, at weight 0.3 if B ranks above A and at weight 0.1 if B ranks below A, the region where the weight table puts the optimum for a highly correlated member. Kaggle's auto-selection marks and the team-rank change after each score reveal which of two equal-looking submissions has the higher unrounded score ([hoshinoyumemi](https://www.kaggle.com/code/hoshinoyumemi/knee-0-939-to-0-956-what-moved-the-score)), but two blends that differ only in our model's weight probably differ by noise of order 0.0005 on about 390 public studies (our estimate), so a single comparison is a nudge, not a verdict.

The final pair exploits the one rule that makes hedging free: Kaggle counts the better of a team's two selected submissions on the private board ([DataCamp](https://campus.datacamp.com/courses/winning-a-kaggle-competition-in-python/modeling?ex=12)). Final 1 therefore only has to hold the floor and Final 2 only has to carry the upside, which means Final 2 can be bolder than a standalone best guess because its downside is already covered. Both must be selected by hand, since Kaggle otherwise selects by public score and could drop the floor. The floor should also not be a fresh fork: exact ties go to the earlier submission, so a late copy of a popular public notebook would land at the back of its cluster, and our existing 0.958 submission or Submission A, whichever ranks higher, is the better choice.

Three risks remain outside the plan. R6 eligibility is open: its manifest has no fastMRI auxiliary entry, but its run name suggests one and heliosli has not answered the direct question asked on 18 Oct ([743416](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743416); [749858](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/749858)), and Kaggle has said it will not pre-approve datasets or weights ([747455](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/747455)). If heliosli confirms fastMRI before Thursday, our own rule removes R6 from both finals, and the public record suggests that a base without R6 or R7 scores only about 0.954, the CoAtNet plus heliosli's S6 ([goodpjw2008 0.958](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-coatnet-r6-2-5d-mil-lb-0-958)). The scanner prior for Lateral OA may not transfer: its gold gain from 0.807 to 0.867 rests on 11 positives, and the host has said prevalence may differ across the train, public and private sets without saying whether the split is stratified by site ([data page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/data); [734681](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/734681)), so keeping it out of Final 1 caps that exposure. Finally, a fallback can be silent: if our group overruns and the kill guard returns the base alone, a submission meant to test our model will look exactly like the floor, so an own-model submission that lands at precisely the floor's rank should be treated as a possible fallback rather than a result.

## Conclusion

The tie block changes what a model is worth. When roughly 1,300 teams submit near-identical predictions, a model's value lies less in its own score than in the size and sign of its difference from the recipe, and in whether that difference is real. That is why the cheapest decision left, a final pair that brackets the cluster, is worth more than any remaining technique: it turns our model from a bet that could cost the medal into an option that can only add. It is also why series TTA is the right last experiment despite its small expected effect, since it is the only remaining change that can be judged offline on 1,146 studies instead of on 58 gold studies or one noisy public submission.

The gap to the top ten was built weeks ago, not in the last 48 hours. Like the top teams before them, the movers since 16 Oct ran second model families trained on image-informed labels, while our model reached the blend late and highly correlated with the public members. In a future contest the lesson is to have a decorrelated second family, already scored on the leaderboard, by the final week, so that the last days bank small, measured inference gains on top of it instead of searching for a +0.009 that only a new model could supply.
