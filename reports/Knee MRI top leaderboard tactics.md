# Break the 0.958 wall with decorrelated models

In the six days left, the team should stop re-weighting public checkpoints and spend its rented H100s on its own five-fold models that differ from the public blend in views, slice coverage and label lineage, using paper-backed noise-modelling methods as the variants that make each run differ. Every public notebook scoring 0.950 or better is a rank blend of the same five or six checkpoints, the best scores **0.958** (a score **411 teams** share), and the family's estimated ceiling is about **0.960**, while the few top-team members who have spoken describe their own five-fold 2.5D CNNs, with the #3 team crediting labels that are "AI, not by hand" and not from reports alone ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard); [0.957 Explained](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/rsna-knee-0-957-explained-why-the-open-ensembl); [discussion 748295](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748295)). The binding constraint is decorrelation, not strength: under a binormal model, an own model scoring 0.950 alone needs a within-class correlation of **≤0.79** with the 0.958 blend to reach the gold line (0.963) and **≤0.71** to reach 0.966, while our CoAtNets, which reproduce the public Raptor pipeline almost slot for slot, sit at a within-class correlation near 0.925 and added at most 0.001. Report-label error splits into omissions (ACL, lateral meniscus, fracture, effusion, synovitis) and severity over-calls (the three OA findings and Baker's cyst), and only such instance-level errors can change an AUC-optimal ranking ([Menon et al. 2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.html)), which is why averaging 31 LLM tables, class-level corrections and same-family out-of-fold relabelling all stalled. The methods aimed squarely at those errors (severity-graded targets, Pi-DUAL privileged-information gates, sparse per-study noise terms and large-loss correction of "not mentioned" negatives) have never been tested on knee MRI or on AUC; we estimate each at 0 to +0.004 alone, with severity grading up to +0.010. By our estimate, a realistic six-day outcome is **0.959–0.963 public**, with the gold line in reach if one own five-fold family lands near 0.955 at low correlation, and 0.966 or better needs nearly every step to work. Since one public submission carries a noise SD of about 0.003, the two final picks should be a robust blend and a blend free of OAI- and fastMRI-trained weights.

Against that picture, our own models have one structural problem. Our CoAtNet uses the same backbone (`coatnet_rmlp_2_rw_384`), the same 140 mm field, the same 96 slices in five slots (26/22/18/12/18), the same 94 inference windows and the same per-finding attention pooling as nartaa's Raptor ([Blend mine in](https://www.kaggle.com/code/heliosli/blend-mine-in-private-lb-luck-to-all-upvoters); [team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)). It is a Raptor without OAI, which is why our distilled students correlate with the public teacher at Spearman 0.958 and moved the blend by at most 0.001. The same audit found that the 96-slot layout keeps only about 65% of slices. Bagging every series was worth **+0.007** public for darcywjoseph, and another participant found that raising slice coverage from 69% to 84% was worth more than any label work ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md); [discussion 743148](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148)). The 192-slice corpus is therefore the right base for every new run.

## Past RSNA winners agree on localise, crop, flip and aggregate

Across the RSNA contests of 2019–2025, five tricks show repeated measured gains of at least 0.005 AUC-equivalent with few counterexamples. Localise-then-crop is the strongest: an ROI crop moved aneurysm 2025's 4th place "from 0.7 to 0.8" and its 5th place by +0.03–0.05, organ-crop models took trauma 2023's 2nd place from 0.39 to 0.35 private log loss, and every top lumbar 2024 team cropped around localised levels ([aneurysm 4th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/4th-place-solution); [aneurysm 5th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/5th-place-solution); [trauma 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/discussion/447453); [lumbar 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/ianpan-kevin-yuji-bartley-2nd-place-solution)). Adjacent slices at native spacing gave aneurysm 4th +0.03 CV and 9th +0.02 CV, while isotropic resampling "substantially worsened" 7th ([aneurysm 9th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/9th-place-solution); [aneurysm 7th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/7th-place-solution)). For aneurysm 2nd, 4× TTA added +0.012 private, and left/right augmentation with a label swap plus 8× TTA a further +0.005 ([aneurysm 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/2nd-place-solution)). Attention-MIL took lumbar 1st from 0.37 to 0.35 public log loss before a bi-LSTM, auxiliary losses and ensembling reached 0.33, and learned aggregation added +0.012 and +0.021 private for aneurysm 15th, whose two-pipeline average added another +0.019 ([lumbar 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/540091); [aneurysm 15th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/15th-place-solution)). Dense auxiliary supervision is the largest lever where masks exist (+0.026 from an auxiliary loss and +0.108 CV from segmentation pretraining for aneurysm 1st), but the knee comes with no masks ([aneurysm 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)).

Translated to the knee, localisation is the strongest argument for our knee-anchored close-up task. heliosli's R6 and R7 already use it (the full field plus 100 mm crops around an anchor, with medial and lateral halves at ±30 mm on coronal) and receive 80% of the Lateral OA weight in the best blend, because "R6 is strong on the lateral compartment" ([goodpjw2008 0.958](https://www.kaggle.com/code/goodpjw2008/rsna-knee-coatnet-r6-2-5d-mil-lb-0-958); [team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)). Two refinements carry over directly from lumbar 2024. The 1st place jittered the localised slice during training at the localiser's own error rate ([lumbar 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/540091)), and the 2nd place averaged 27 crops per target (3 slices × 9 in-plane offsets), which it found "significantly" better than a single crop ([lumbar 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/ianpan-kevin-yuji-bartley-2nd-place-solution)). The 3rd place and Claudio Verdú Ruiz, now at 0.964 here, trained levels and sides as separate rows of one shared model ([lumbar 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539453); [lumbar 13th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539510)). The knee analogue is one compartment head that sees medial and lateral crops as separate rows with a side embedding, so the under-called lateral findings borrow strength from medial positives, which are more frequent in our report labels (medial meniscus 46% vs lateral 23%) ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)). That design is our inference, not a measured knee result.

Flip is subtler than it looks. nartaa's anatomical mirror (reversed sagittal slice order, width-flipped coronal and axial images, labels unchanged) added +0.001, while heliosli's flip TTA swaps the medial and lateral outputs back after mirroring and takes MCL from the plain pass only ([discussion 746792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792); [Blend mine in](https://www.kaggle.com/code/heliosli/blend-mine-in-private-lb-luck-to-all-upvoters)). By our reading of the anatomy, both are correct for their pipelines. A model that sees left and right knees in native orientation treats a mirrored right knee as a plausible left knee, so its labels survive the flip. A pipeline that canonicalises laterality, as the left/right side classifier shipped in heliosli's bundle suggests, must swap paired outputs, and MCL then has no labelled lateral twin ([R7 manifest](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/heliosli/rsna-knee-compact-r7cnxfsss)). Our plain mirror TTA gave nothing ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)), which is what the first case predicts for a model that has already seen both sides. Once our close-ups are laterality-canonical, a mirror with medial↔lateral swaps of the meniscus and OA labels becomes a genuine doubling of training data for the compartment heads, the knee version of Theo Viel's target-flipping augmentation ([aneurysm 6th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/discussion/611925)).

No Kaggle winner trained on report-mined labels, but HMS 2024 is the closest precedent for labels whose quality varies by sample. All of its top four trained on everything, or with vote-weighted loss, and then fine-tuned or validated only on high-agreement samples; the 2nd place weighted the loss by voters/20 for 15 epochs, then trained 5 epochs on samples with at least six votes at a 10× lower learning rate ([HMS 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/discussion/492560); [HMS 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/discussion/492254)). Agreement across our 31 tables plays the part of the vote count, and HMS 3rd's observation that pretrained 2D models gained nothing from low-vote data ([HMS 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/hms-harmful-brain-activity-classification/discussion/492471)) favours a short final stage on high-agreement cells over heavy reweighting. The cautionary result comes from aneurysm 11th, the design closest to ours (2.5D `coatnet_rmlp_2_rw_384` with recurrent heads): self-distillation on out-of-fold logits raised CV by **+0.0126** while costing **−0.0099** public and **−0.0088** private ([aneurysm 11th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/11th-place-solution)). Runtime is the other recurring lesson. Four aneurysm write-ups below 6th place report submissions lost to timeouts, and the 11th place fitted 11 backbones plus 45 recurrent heads into 12 hours on 2×T4 by sharing GPU preprocessing and running cheap heads over cached slice features ([aneurysm 8th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/8th-place-solution); [aneurysm 11th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/11th-place-solution)).

## Report labels carry two kinds of error that table averaging cannot remove

The host's labels follow a different standard from the reports. Gold and test labels come from image review by two independent readers with a third adjudicating, the image wins when it disagrees with the report, and borderline findings are graded negative "to favor specificity" ([discussion 733491](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733491); [discussion 733343](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733343)). The rubric's thresholds are strict: OA requires "a moderate or large area (roughly 1 cm or greater) of high-grade cartilage loss (>50% thickness)", effusion and Baker's cyst must be moderate or large, MCL must be a high-grade acute tear, and a meniscal tear must definitely reach the surface on at least two images ([discussion 733343](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733343)). The prevalence gap between our report labels and gold-58 shows the two resulting error types. Findings that reports omit are more common on gold (**ACL 41% vs 23%, lateral meniscus 40% vs 23%, fracture 31% vs 18%**, effusion 60% vs 50%, synovitis 47% vs 39%), while findings that reports call at milder grades are rarer on gold (**medial OA 26% vs 37%, lateral OA 19% vs 29%, Baker's cyst 21% vs 31%**, PF OA 36% vs 44%) ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)). Gold may be an enriched sample, so the direction of each gap is more reliable than its size.

Theory says which of these errors can matter for AUC. If the flip rates a = P(report negative | image positive) and b = P(report positive | image negative) do not depend on the study, then P(report positive | x) = b + (1 − a − b)·P(image positive | x), which rises strictly with P(image positive | x) whenever a + b < 1; the AUC-optimal ranking under report labels is then the AUC-optimal ranking under image labels ([Menon et al. 2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.html)). Class-level corrections such as Gold Loss Correction, forward or backward transition matrices and PU class-prior fixes therefore cannot raise asymptotic AUC. Only errors that vary with the image move the ranking: a mild-OA knee called positive, a subtle tear left unreported. Adding tables cannot remove those either, because every table reads the same report. Among 71 HELM-evaluated language models, two models that both err choose the same wrong answer **60%** of the time on average, and more accurate models are more correlated ([Kim et al. 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html)); nine LLM judges supply about **two effective votes**, and the best single judge matches the panel ([Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29800)). Our gold numbers agree, with the mean of 17 public tables at **0.899** and the best single table at **0.904** ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)), and a comparison of eight public sets found no clearly better set for 11 of 12 findings ([discussion 747540](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/747540)). Label models that assume independent annotators, such as Dawid–Skene or Snorkel, should land on the soft mean for the same reason.

Where the forum measured instance-level fixes, they hit exactly those errors. Reports leave **83.7%** of synovitis cells "not addressed"; filling only the undecided synovitis cells from the effusion field lifted the gold synovitis column from **0.678 to 0.790**, whereas blanket imputation across all findings cost Baker's 0.029 and contusion 0.031, because silence means absence for Baker's (3% positive when silent) but not for synovitis (34%) ([discussion 733932](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733932)). darcywjoseph's per-finding public probe put a 0.948 model at **0.890 on PF OA, 0.896 on lateral OA and 0.932 on synovitis**, 0.956 on lateral meniscus and a mean of 0.963 on the other eight findings. Every report extractor he tried made the same five lateral-OA false positives on gold ("No prompt can correct that"), and PF OA looked image-limited, with text at 0.96 on gold, every image model near 0.89, and neither resolution nor compartment crops helping ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md)). An agreement filter that trained only on cells where the tables agree above 0.9 or below 0.1 dropped about 25% of cells and "improved my results on CV and public LB" ([discussion 745861](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745861)).

Our two label experiments failed in the way the literature predicts. BCE is linear in its target, so training on 0.6 × OOF prediction + 0.4 × report label is identical to a 60% self-distillation loss, and our OOF teachers were the same architecture on the same series and labels, each trained on 75% of the data and weaker on gold (0.904–0.914) than the final model ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)). A student without added noise or new data stays at its baseline (**83.2% vs 83.3%** in Noisy Student's ablation) ([Xie et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04252)); a weak machine annotator drags its student below the baseline (**76.1% vs 77.5%** in ReLabel) ([Yun et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05022)); and bootstrapping mixes in only 5–20% of a model's own prediction ([Reed et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6596)). The distilled students failed for a neighbouring reason. The public teacher's predictions on our training studies are in-sample, so the students became copies, and distillation transfers a teacher's features without adding new ones ([Allen-Zhu & Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09816)).

The verdicts themselves were also too noisy to trust. At AUC 0.90, one finding's AUC on 58 studies has a standard error of 0.04–0.07, and the paired macro difference between two models has an SE of about 0.006–0.017 (our computation with the [Hanley–McNeil](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiology.143.1.7063747) formula); the OOF-mixed model's gold score of 0.918 sits inside the 0.915–0.927 spread of our plain report-label seeds. colum2131 measured gold-58 against public LB at r = 0.66 over 15 submissions, against r = 0.87 for out-of-fold AUC on the ~4,300 LLM-labelled studies ([discussion 748011](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748011)). The OOF-mixed model does not appear among our leaderboard submissions ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)), so the image-informed-label direction the top teams use has not yet been tested where it counts. Choosing SWA epochs on gold-58 should stop too, in favour of EMA or a fixed late-epoch average chosen on full out-of-fold AUC. Tucker's team trains on gold at about 1.3% of the loss and darcywjoseph at 3× weight ([discussion 735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304); [SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md)), which is a more productive use of 58 studies than a validation set that cannot resolve the differences at stake.

Several popular tools are predicted to add nothing here. DivideMix lost **12.9 AUC points** on real report-derived chest X-ray noise, where ELR gained about 1 and NVUM about 2.5 ([BoMD benchmark](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.01937)); on real partial labels, blanket down-weighting or smoothing of "not mentioned = negative" lost **5–7 mAP** ([Kim et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03740)). Deep AUC maximisation beat the best cross-entropy ensemble on CheXpert, the closest public analogue (report-trained, expert-tested, mean AUC), by only **+0.0006** (0.9305 vs 0.9299), and its larger gains came at 1–2% prevalence ([LibAUC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03173)); our prevalences run from 16% to 60%. Standard TTA cut MedMNIST accuracy by up to 31.6 points ([arXiv 2604.09697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09697)), and re-estimating BatchNorm statistics on test batches can fail catastrophically under label shift ([arXiv 2402.04958](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04958v2)), which the host warns may exist between train, public and private sets ([data page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/data)).

## Noise-modelling papers buy decorrelation more than accuracy

The methods below come from the noisy-label, privileged-information and self-training literature and target the two error types above. None appears in any public knee notebook, and none has been tested on report-derived MRI labels or with ROC-AUC as the metric, so every expected gain in this section is our estimate. Their main value is a guaranteed difference: each changes which cells the image features are allowed to memorise, so a run trained with it errs differently from the public blend, which is exactly the property the decorrelation arithmetic in the last section says we lack.

### Severity grades attack the over-calls that no prompt fixes

The best-evidenced idea is also the one with in-competition precedent. heliosli's compact ConvNeXts, the strongest public ConvNeXt models, already use it: R7's manifest lists a graded-state head (weight 0.5) trained on report-derived severity categories and an "extent" auxiliary for the three OA findings (weight 0.2), and both R7 and R6 ship Effusion and Baker's state stackers ([R7 manifest](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/heliosli/rsna-knee-compact-r7cnxfsss); [R6 manifest](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/heliosli/rsna-knee-r6-frozen-fe055406-w1)). The papers point the same way: on CheXpert, mapping hedged mentions to soft LSR values beat hard U-Zeros and U-Ones by **+1.3 to +1.4** mean AUC ([Pham et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.06475)), and image-aligned labels raised image-model AUROC from 0.84 to 0.88, with **+0.14 and +0.16** on the two hedge-prone findings ([Jain et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.00793)). The recipe runs a local LLM only on cells whose 31-table mean is at least 0.2, so presence still comes from the consensus and the LLM only grades. The vocabulary is closed and written from the host rubric: OA per compartment as none, mild, moderate or high-grade; effusion as none, trace, small, moderate or large; meniscus as definite surface tear, possible tear or intrasubstance signal; ACL and MCL as complete or high-grade, low-grade or sprain, or mucoid or chronic; fracture as acute, old or insufficiency; contusion as traumatic or degenerative. The target becomes consensus × g(grade), with g at 0.1–0.3 below threshold, 0.6–0.7 for an ungraded mention and 0.9–1.0 at or above threshold, and a cumulative-logit head whose P(grade ≥ threshold) is rank-averaged with the main head. The map g must never be fitted on gold-58, whose per-finding noise is about ±0.06. Our estimate is +0.003 to +0.010 macro, concentrated on lateral, medial and PF OA and Baker's; raising lateral OA alone from 0.79 to 0.85 is worth 0.005 macro. The obstacle is our own working rule of using only the 31 extracted tables. The host has ruled that even sending report text to commercial LLM APIs for label extraction is allowed and is not "private sharing" ([discussion 733965](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733965)), so a local model on rented hardware is within the competition rules. If the team keeps its rule, the first step is to check whether any public table already carries grade fields.

### Model the omissions instead of relabelling them

For the under-called findings the literature offers three loss-level tools that never inject teacher predictions into targets, which is where our relabelling went wrong. Large-loss correction treats "not mentioned" cells as negatives, lets the network learn for an epoch, then flips the target of the highest-loss assumed negatives; on real OpenImages partial labels it moved mAP from 82.0 to 82.6, while blanket down-weighting lost 5 points ([Kim et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03740)). For us it belongs only on ACL, lateral meniscus and fracture cells whose table mean is below 0.1, starting at epoch 2 and growing the corrected share by about 0.2% per epoch to a 3% cap, with positives never touched, since the paper itself shows correction precision falling as epochs pass. SOP adds a sparse per-sample, per-class noise term s = u²·y − v²·(1 − y), learned alongside the network and discarded at test; it reached 93.24% on CIFAR-10N "worst" against 77.69% for cross-entropy, and 73.5% on Clothing1M ([Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.14026)). Our adaptation, untested, enables only the negative (omission) side on the six under-called findings and gives u and v a 10–100× learning rate, because each study is seen only 16 times. Pi-DUAL mixes, in logit space, an image-only prediction network with a noise network fed privileged information, through a gate driven by that same information, and keeps only the image network at test. On ImageNet relabelled by 16 pretrained models, the closest public analogue to our LLM tables, it scored **62.1% against 47.2%** for cross-entropy at high noise and 71.6% against 68.2% at low noise, for 11% extra step time ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06600)).

The privileged information must never be the labels. Feeding the noisy label as privileged input collapsed TRAM from 55.0% to **38.3%** on CIFAR-10H and approximate marginalisation to **0.8%** on ImageNet-PI ([Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01806)). Safe inputs are label-agnostic: per-cell table disagreement 4m(1 − m), a sign-free pattern of which LLM families dissent, site and scanner tags from the 86 retained DICOM fields, and a random per-study ID vector of length 8–30. Routing site tags to the noise path is deliberate, because a site's habit of terse reports is label noise, and an image head that saw the tags would reproduce it at test. The only medical data point is instructive: on 3,055 chest X-rays with 14 raters, raw multi-rater input made TRAM worse than plain fine-tuning (**0.842 vs 0.856** AUROC), while supplying it only on high-uncertainty cases made it better (**0.872**) ([Gao et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10885868/)). Before training, one CPU hour on gold-58 settles the design. Measure the AUROC with which disagreement, dissent and metadata flag report-vs-gold errors, and the share of erroneous cells that are confident (table mean below 0.1 or above 0.9). Below about 0.6, keep only the random ID; if most errors sit in confident cells, SOP and the random-ID path are the levers, because no disagreement-based gate can see them. Instance-level noise is not identifiable from the tables alone, since identification needs three conditionally independent labels and our 31 share one report ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02016)); these methods work through memorisation dynamics instead. None of them fixes the over-calls, which are systematic and easy to fit, and that is the job of severity grades.

### Give image evidence a different teacher and a narrow door

Image-informed labels remain the strongest forum signal, and the evidence specifies what would make our failed loop work: new information in the teacher, a narrow scope, a small weight, a noised student and one round. Co-training needs views that are each sufficient and conditionally independent given the label ([Qiao et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05984)); multi-planar co-training on CT fused axial, coronal and sagittal teachers into pseudo-labels and beat full supervision by more than 4%, and its authors fuse planes precisely because a single view's pseudo-label errors "will be reinforced" over iterations ([Zhou et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02586); [Xia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12506)). For us that means teachers trained on different planes or backbones, only on confident cells, and cross-fitted. Fills go only to the consensus-masked uncertain cells (12% of cells) and to silent cells of omission-prone findings; confident cells move at most 10–20%, and only where two sufficient views agree. A gate of at most about 16 parameters, fitted to gold-58 by leave-one-study-out (pooled slopes on the report logit, a cross-view image score and table disagreement, plus per-finding intercepts), decides per finding group whether image evidence may change labels at all. Veit et al. showed the idea works with verified labels on only 0.4% of the data (AP_all 83.82 → 87.67) ([Veit et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01619)). The student should be equal or larger, start from ImageNet rather than teacher weights, use strong augmentation, stochastic depth and dropout, draw at least half of each batch from unmodified cells ([Arazo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983)), and send filled cells to a separate pseudo head that is dropped at inference, as in Debiased Self-Training ([Chen et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.07136)). A CPU-only companion needs no raw text: a gradient-boosted map from the full 31-table × 12-finding vector to the cross-fitted image score, which keeps cross-finding cues such as effusion → synovitis and works as a text-free cousin of VisualCheXbert, the model that learned to map reports onto image-model outputs ([Jain et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11467)). We expect 0 to +0.004 public. On rented H100s the minimal version (one extra teacher family on axial and coronal series, four folds, fills only in masked cells, one student) costs about 1.5 GPU-days by our estimate.

### Three cheap bets for idle slots

Three further ideas deserve a run only when a GPU would otherwise sit idle. Doctor Net modelled 31 diabetic-retinopathy graders with separate heads and averaged them at test, beating a model of the average grader (binary AUC 97.11 → 97.28) ([Guan et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08774)); our 31 "graders" read one report, so the gain will be far smaller. A two-stage AUC-margin fine-tune is worth one ensemble member for its diversity rather than its own gain ([LibAUC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03173)). Local vision-language models are weak knee graders: MedGemma 1.5 4B reached 64.7% balanced accuracy on an MRI benchmark that includes knee meniscal tears and fractures and Qwen3-VL-4B 49.6% ([MedGemma 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v2)), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B graded knee MOAKS at 0.47 zero-shot ([3DReasonKnee](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20967v1)). Use one only as a gated input for fluid-visible findings, and note that a student trained on MedGemma outputs may count as a "Model Derivative" under Google's HAI-DEF terms ([HAI-DEF terms](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms)).

## Placement and a second CNN family beat a foundation encoder

No source shows a newer general backbone beating a fine-tuned ImageNet-12k CoAtNet on knee MRI, and the top lumbar-spine 2024 solutions used ordinary ImageNet-family timm backbones behind a localisation stage ([lumbar 2nd repository](https://github.com/brendanartley/RSNA-2024-Competition); [lumbar 3rd repository](https://github.com/Moyasii/Kaggle-2024-RSNA-Pub)). Inside this competition, the evidence favours cheaper, different CNNs over a bigger CoAtNet. azamat1ch's five-fold ConvNeXt-T beat his CoAtNet by 0.007 ([discussion 735304](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304)); laymond found 384 → 288 px "barely had any effect"; and darcywjoseph found 384 vs 320 px and ConvNeXt-S vs T level ([discussion 743148](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148); [SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/darcywjoseph/Knee-Abnormality-Detection/blob/HEAD/SOLUTION.md)). CoAtNet-rmlp-2 is the resolution-bound exception, losing 0.029 at 256 px ([discussion 737696](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/737696)). The second family should therefore be ConvNeXt-T at 224–288 px, the strongest reported five-fold result and cheap at inference, with EfficientNetV2-S as a third if capacity allows, since it is architecturally furthest from both the CoAtNet and the ConvNeXt public families. The licence-clean option at 384 px is `convnext_base.clip_laion2b_augreg_ft_in12k_in1k_384` (Apache-2.0), which runs at **1.76×** CoAtNet-2-384's throughput on the same GPU ([model card](https://huggingface.co/timm/convnext_base.clip_laion2b_augreg_ft_in12k_in1k_384); [timm benchmark](https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt291-cu128-4090.csv)). DINOv3 ConvNeXt weights cost the same and are ungated in timm, but carry a custom licence that one DrivenData competition ruled ineligible for prizes ([DrivenData forum](https://community.drivendata.org/t/is-dinov3-allowed-to-use/11488)).

Medical foundation encoders do not change that calculus. OrthoFoundation-L is the only released 2D encoder with large-scale knee-MRI pretraining (DINOv3-L continued on 1.25M knee images, reporting ACL AUROC 98.49 and cartilage-lesion AUROC 93.38 after fine-tuning), but its pretraining includes about 200k OAI MRI slices and 300k fastMRI slices, and its README lists no baselines ([OrthoFoundation](https://github.com/ytrsk/OrthoFoundation)). PRISM/MARS and OrthoDiffusion are 3D models compared only with from-scratch 3D baselines, at MRNet accuracy 80.05% vs 77.78% and macro-AUC 90.79 vs 83.15 ([PRISM](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07165v2); [OrthoDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20752v1)), and RadImageNet's advantage was not confirmed on ACL tears by a 2025 study or across seven tasks by another ([Frees et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17567); [Juodelyte et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08272)). At 4,349 labelled studies, our estimate for swapping in a domain encoder is at most +0.005 single-model AUC. OrthoFoundation is worth one experiment on 224 px close-ups, kept only if it beats our own close-up model by at least 0.01 out-of-fold on the weak findings, and only after the OAI question has been raised with the host.

Rented H100s make in-domain self-supervised pretraining affordable, but the evidence caps its value. Contrastive pretraining on CheXpert's own training images raised mean AUC from 0.7625 (supervised ImageNet) to 0.7729, and same-patient positives added +1.18 top-1 over SimCLR in dermatology ([Azizi et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.05224)); same-study positives were also the best pairing in MedAug ([MedAug](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10663)). Continued pretraining converges in about 5k steps and then overfits small datasets, and starting it from a supervised base was "considerably worse" than from a self-supervised one ([HPT, Reed et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.12718)); our CoAtNet is supervised. No verified Kaggle medical solution has reported a quantified gain from image SSL on competition images. If the team runs it anyway, the recipe is DINO-style self-distillation at 224 px with fine-tuning at 384, one frozen-backbone epoch to warm the head, layer-wise learning-rate decay, 20–50 epochs with checkpoints every 5–10, and a cross-sequence positive (the nearest same-plane slice from the other series of the same study) as the second global crop with probability 0.3–0.5. Our estimate, scaled from the DINO reference timings and timm throughput, is about 100 A100-hours for CoAtNet-2 at 224 px, roughly 7–8 hours on 8×H100, with the data loader as the real bottleneck ([DINO repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dino)). Promote it to five folds only if two folds gain at least 0.003 macro AUC or add clear decorrelation.

Localisation is where compute buys the most. The public R6, R7 and S6 models place their close-ups with an SKM-TEA-trained anatomy V-Net shipped in heliosli's orthofoundation dataset ([discussion 749858](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/749858)), and reusing it is the fastest route for our close-up task. SKM-TEA's exact access terms were not checked in this research, but datasets that need only registration and acceptance of licence terms are what the host's 14 Oct statement calls clearly acceptable ([discussion 743416](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743416)). A licence-clean upgrade is TotalSegmentator's `total_mr` task (Apache-2.0), which segments the femur at Dice **0.897** on clinical knee MRI but leaves tibia and patella to a separately licensed task ([TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator); [arXiv 2508.06076](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06076)). The femur alone defines every anchor we need: the condylar joint line, the intercondylar notch as the medial/lateral split, the trochlear groove for the patellofemoral crop, the posterior condyles for Baker's cyst and the posterior horns, and the suprapatellar recess for effusion and synovitis. Run it offline on the rented GPUs, preferring non-fat-suppressed series where marrow signal is intact, check 50 studies by eye, and distil the landmarks into a small regressor such as `convnext_nano`, which runs at 15.3× CoAtNet's throughput ([timm benchmark](https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models/blob/main/results/benchmark-infer-amp-nchw-pt291-cu128-4090.csv)); lumbar 2024's 2nd place put 99.1% of its keypoints within 5 px with such a stage ([Artley repository](https://github.com/brendanartley/RSNA-2024-Competition)). Keep TotalSegmentator itself out of the Kaggle kernel: at an assumed 10–30 s per series it would need 7–33 GPU-hours for 1,300 studies (our arithmetic).

External data is not worth any of the six days. No 2025–2026 public knee dataset with compartment OA, synovitis or side-specific meniscus labels has been released; OAI with MOAKS grades covers all four weak findings and lifted nartaa from 0.942 to 0.945 ([discussion 746792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/746792)), but whether geo-restricted NIH registration satisfies rule 2.6.b is still unanswered ([discussion 749145](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/749145)), and so is fastMRI's redistribution clause ([discussion 748480](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748480)). Members of two teams at 0.964 or better, azamatt and knee-deep, say they used none ([discussion 748295](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748295); [discussion 743416](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743416)).

## A six-day schedule that spends slots only on decorrelated members

With rented H100s the bottleneck moves from GPU-hours to decisions per day and Kaggle slots, five a day or about 30 in total. Training on rented machines the team controls sits comfortably within the rules as the host has interpreted them, since the host ruled that even sending report text to commercial LLM APIs is not "private sharing" ([discussion 733965](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733965)); keep the data in private storage and delete it afterwards. The H100s should carry the five-fold runs and their variants, the local 4070 Super and L4 should finish the 192-slice CoAtNet runs and the CPU-side diagnostics, and Kaggle slots should go only to blends whose new member has passed a decorrelation check.

That check is where the arithmetic bites. The table gives, under a binormal model with optimal weights ([sensitivity index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_index)), the largest within-class correlation with the 0.958 public blend at which an own model of a given standalone AUC lifts the blend to each target (our computation). The values are upper bounds, because real errors cluster on shared hard cases: our 0.925-correlated students were predicted to add up to +0.003 and delivered at most 0.001. Public models correlate with each other at about 0.86–0.89.

Before a new member gets a slot, measure its within-class Spearman with the current blend, separately for positives and negatives, on gold-58 and on training studies where both are out-of-sample, together with its pair-fix rate: the share of positive–negative pairs the blend mis-orders that the candidate orders correctly, minus the pairs it breaks. Only candidates near or below 0.85 with a positive pair-fix rate deserve a submission. Blend by per-finding rank averaging with global weights (equal among strong members, 0.2–0.35 for a decorrelated weaker one), shrink any per-finding deviation at least 70% toward the global weight, and never hill-climb on gold-58. In our own split-half test plain averaging beat best-per-finding selection (+0.0075 vs +0.0064) ([team brief](../DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md)), matching the forecast-combination finding that estimated weights lose to simple averages ([Qian et al.](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/on-the-forecast-combination-puzzle/)). A weak member at high weight does damage; by the same binormal model, a 0.93 member at ρ = 0.85 blended 50/50 costs about 0.005.

Decode each series once and share it across members, normalise uint8 batches on the GPU, put one model family on each T4, and add cheap heads over cached slice embeddings rather than more backbones ([aneurysm 11th](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/11th-place-solution)). Use fp16, not bf16: the T4 emulates bf16, and one participant's time guard turned a 0.851 model into 0.710 ([discussion 744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230)). Ship pylibjpeg or GDCM for the JPEG Lossless and JPEG 2000 transfer syntaxes the data page lists ([discussion 747865](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/747865)), and follow Chris Deotte's advice to commit a notebook that infers 1,300 random training studies, which surfaces NaNs and malformed DICOMs before the final day ([discussion 749623](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/749623)). TorchScript or TensorRT export gave CZII's winners a 2–3× speed-up but adds failure risk this late ([CZII 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/czii-cryo-et-object-identification/discussion/561510)).

Pick the two finals by priors and consistency rather than by the single best public number. With one-submission noise of about 0.003, the best of K near-equal submissions is inflated by about +0.0035 for K = 5, +0.0046 for K = 10 and +0.0061 for K = 30, and by roughly half as much for correlated variants (our computation of the optimizer's curse ([Smith & Winkler 2006](https://fds.duke.edu/db/aas/stat/faculty/bob.winkler/publications/278471))). The SIIM-ISIC 2020 winners averaged 18 models' probability ranks, "did not use public LB score feedback in any way", and won private at 0.9490 ([Ha et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.05351)); Kaggle staff warned in this competition that low-effort ensembles "tend to drop on the private leaderboard" ([discussion 748181](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/748181)). Kaggle's auto-selection markers reveal which of two equal-looking submissions has the higher unrounded score ([hoshinoyumemi](https://www.kaggle.com/code/hoshinoyumemi/knee-0-939-to-0-956-what-moved-the-score)). The first pick should be the strongest blend with shrunk per-finding weights, because heliosli's raw weights have no stated basis and the notebook contains a disabled single-class probe switch ([Blend mine in](https://www.kaggle.com/code/heliosli/blend-mine-in-private-lb-luck-to-all-upvoters)). The second pick should contain no OAI- or fastMRI-trained component (nartaa's all-data S6, heliosli's S6, goodpjw2008's reader and our own models), which protects prize eligibility if OAI is ruled out; the host requires winners to list every public weight they used and its source data ([discussion 744056](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744056)).

## Conclusion

This competition is decided by decorrelation, and that changes what "novel" is worth here. The paper-backed methods the forum has not tried are unmeasured on knee MRI, their estimated gains run to a few thousandths each and overlap, so together they will not plausibly supply the missing +0.013; what each one does guarantee is a model that fails differently from the public family, and difference is what every null result so far has lacked. The same lens exposes the team's most expensive constraint, which is self-imposed: the rule of using only the 31 extracted tables blocks severity grading, the one label lever with a precedent in the strongest public compact models and the one aimed at the OA findings where every reported model is weakest, although the host permits the local-LLM pass it requires.

The target itself deserves a second look. The top 30 teams have a median of 161 submissions ([leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard)), so their displayed scores are maxima over many correlated variants, and at a noise SD of 0.003 the best of about 160 half-correlated variants is inflated by roughly +0.004 (our computation), so by the same arithmetic the private board should compress toward the low 0.96s. A blend whose true quality is near 0.962, which the decorrelation table puts within reach if one own five-fold family lands near 0.955 at a within-class correlation around 0.8, is therefore a credible top-10 private candidate even if its public score never shows 0.969. The practical objective for the six days is that robust 0.962, with a public 0.969 as a by-product at best, and gold-58 serves it best as a harm check or as a small slice of the training loss, never as the judge.
