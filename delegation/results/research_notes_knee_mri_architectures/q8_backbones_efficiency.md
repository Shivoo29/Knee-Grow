# Efficient Backbones & Training Recipes vs 2.5D CNN Baseline (Knee MRI)

## Which efficient backbones beat CoAtNet/ResNet 2.5D baselines on knee or similar MRI with measured deltas?

### Takeaway
No head-to-head study of ConvNeXt/EfficientNetV2/MaxViT vs a CoAtNet 2.5D-window baseline on knee MRI was found; the strongest directly measured knee-MRI deltas come from domain pretraining (RadImageNet +4.5–4.8% AUC on ACL/meniscus) and from MRNet-era architecture+augmentation upgrades (up to ~93.4% val AUC), while ImageNet-scale efficiency numbers favor EfficientNetV2-S and ConvNeXt-T/S as the cheapest CoAtNet replacements.

### Cited Findings
- CoAtNet (NeurIPS 2021) reaches 86.0% ImageNet top-1 with 1K-only training (CoAtNet-3); with ImageNet-21K pretraining the best variant hits 88.56%, matching ViT-H/14 trained on 23x more data; scaled to JFT-3B it sets 90.88% — the accuracy bar any efficient replacement is judged against, at 75M–688M params and 96–812 GFLOPs depending on variant. — [NeurIPS paper (PDF)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/20568692db622456cc42a2e853ca21f8-Paper.pdf); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04803v2)
- ConvNeXt (CVPR 2022): ConvNeXt-T 82.1% top-1 with 28M params / 4.5 GFLOPs, ConvNeXt-S 83.1% / 50M / 8.7G, ConvNeXt-B 83.8% / 89M / 15.4G at 224px; ConvNeXt-B at 384px beats Swin-B 85.1% vs 84.5% (+0.6%) with 12.5% higher inference throughput (95.7 vs 85.1 img/s), and the throughput edge grows with resolution — directly relevant to 384px 2.5D windows; ConvNeXt-XL reaches 87.8%. — [CVPR Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Liu_A_ConvNet_for_the_2020s_CVPR_2022_paper.html); [Official checkpoints table](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ConvNeXt/)
- EfficientNetV2 (ICML/PMLR 2021): EfficientNetV2-S 83.9% (22M, 8.8B FLOPs), -M 85.1% (54M, 24B), -L 85.7% (120M, 53B); with 21K pretraining -XL hits 87.3%; trains up to 11x faster than EfficientNet-B7 at matched accuracy and is up to 6.8x smaller; EfficientNetV2-M matches B7 accuracy training 11x faster; progressive resizing+regularization is part of the speedup. — [PMLR proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/tan21a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00298)
- MaxViT (ECCV 2022): MaxViT-T 83.62% (31M, 5.6G), -S 84.45% (69M, 11.7G), -B 84.95% (120M, 23.4G), -L 85.17% (212M, 43.9G) at 224px without extra data (SOTA at the time, 86.5% family best 1K-only); at 512px MaxViT-L hits 86.70%; with 21K pretraining MaxViT-XL hits 88.70%; MaxViT-L at 224px beats CoAtNet-3 by +0.67%, and 21K MaxViT-B beats CoAtNet-4 by +0.28% using only 43% of params and 38% of FLOPs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01697v4); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/maxvit-multi-axis-vision-transformer/)
- MRNet knee baselines (the reference 2.5D-style CNN regime): original MRNet (Bien et al.) reports AUC 0.937 abnormal / 0.965 ACL / 0.847 meniscus on internal validation, and 0.824 on external ACL transfer without retraining (0.911 after retraining); public leaderboard best single model averages 0.917 across the three tasks. — [MRNet paper (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258509); [MRNet leaderboard](https://stanfordmlgroup.github.io/competitions/mrnet)
- MRNet replication (Azcona): swapping in more recent architectures plus tuned augmentation reaches 93.4% validation AUC, with transfer learning and augmentation identified as the decisive factors rather than any single backbone. — [Azcona MRNet study (GitHub)](https://github.com/dazcona/mrnet); [Thesis PDF](https://doras.dcu.ie/25068/1/David_Azcona_MRNet.pdf)
- ConvNeXt on brain MRI classification: ConvNeXt-Base hits 98.37% accuracy / 0.992 AUC (coronal plane) on Alzheimer's MRI; an improved lightweight ConvNeXt-Tiny reaches 89.09% accuracy and per-class AUCs 0.97–0.98 under CPU-only training; EfficientNetV2-based brain-MRI classifiers report 99.16–99.76% accuracy in two 2024–2025 studies (small single-center sets — treat as optimistic). — [Diagnostics 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4418/15/23/2997); [arXiv 2508.11532](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11532v1); [EfficientNetV2+attention brain MRI (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10586-024-04532-1); [EfficientNetV2 brain tumor (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010482524016275)
- MedNeXt (MICCAI 2023) shows a fully-ConvNeXt 3D medical backbone scales down to 5.6M params (Small) – evidence ConvNeXt blocks are viable at low parameter budgets for volumetric medical data. — [MedNeXt GitHub](https://github.com/mic-dkfz/mednext)
- Multi-scale-fusion MaxViT for medical image classification (2025) and MaxViT-UNet (2023, hybrid MaxViT encoder + hybrid decoder) report gains over vanilla UNet/Swin-UNet on medical segmentation, supporting MaxViT as a hybrid candidate — but neither reports a knee-MRI 2.5D classification delta vs CoAtNet/ResNet. — [MSF-MaxViT (MDPI Electronics 2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/5/912); [MaxViT-UNet (arXiv 2305.08396)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08396)

### Inferences
- For a 12–24 GB single GPU at 384px with 2.5D windows, ConvNeXt-T (28M) and EfficientNetV2-S (22M) are the cheapest credible CoAtNet challengers on throughput/memory grounds; MaxViT-T/S cost more per step but buy built-in global context that may matter for multi-finding (12-label) reasoning.
- The absence of any published CoAtNet-vs-ConvNeXt/EfficientNetV2/MaxViT knee-MRI bake-off means backbone-swap deltas for this task must be measured in-house; ImageNet deltas (+0.3–0.7% between neighbors) suggest single-backbone swaps alone are unlikely to deliver more than ~1–2% macro-AUC — UNVERIFIED estimate.

### Gaps
- No paper, notebook, or Kaggle RSNA-knee-2026 forum post found with a measured ConvNeXt / EfficientNetV2 / MaxViT vs CoAtNet (or ResNet) delta on knee MRI macro-AUC; RSNA 2026 Kaggle forum backbone comparisons could not be verified — no citable numbers.
- No 2.5D-window-specific FLOPs/memory measurements at 384px for these backbones found; timm is the standard source of such weights/benchmarks but no timm doc page with knee-relevant numbers was captured.
- DINOv3: no verified publication or weights found during this research; excluded from recommendations.

## Frozen early blocks + head training: how much is retained, how much faster?

### Takeaway
Freezing the backbone and training only a head retains ~96–99% of full-fine-tune AUC on large radiology classification sets and enables PEFT variants (<1% of params) to match full tuning; the RadImageNet study itself found full unfreezing best on average but at full training cost, so the practical recipe is frozen/PEFT warm-start followed by a short full-unfreeze at low LR.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv2 radiology benchmark (Baharoon et al., arXiv 2312.02366; journal version in Medical Image Analysis): on ViT-g/14, linear probing (frozen backbone, ~1,500 trainable params) reaches 0.759 vs 0.769 end-to-end on NIH Chest X-ray (~98.7% retained) and 0.818 vs 0.848 on CheXpert (~96.5% retained); LoRA (8M params, 0.7%) reaches 0.767/0.823 and BitFit (0.8M, 0.07%) reaches 0.768/0.817 — both within ~0.005–0.03 of full 1,100M-param fine-tuning. — [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v3); [HuggingFace paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2312.02366)
- Same study on segmentation with frozen DINOv2 encoder + trainable U-Net decoder (17M/5% of ViT-L, 38M/3% of ViT-g): beats from-scratch U-Net and TransUnet (324M trained 100%) on the hard AMOS multi-organ task, and matches them on easy lung data — evidence frozen-encoder + decoder-only training wins when labels are scarce. — [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v3)
- RadImageNet original study (Mei et al., Radiology: Artificial Intelligence 2022 — venue is RSNA's Radiology: AI, NOT NeurIPS): across 24 fine-tune scenarios per task, unfreezing all layers consistently beat partial-freeze and head-only settings; LR 0.0001 recommended when training all parameters. So head-only is cheaper but not better on average in their protocol. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9530758)
- Stanford CS231N 2024 knee/breast study: partial unfreezing (5 backbone layers) with SGD+momentum+strong weight decay was optimal in their grid; head-only underfit and full-unfreeze at high LR overfit on the small sets; their best ACL model hit 0.9969 test AUC. — [CS231N paper PDF](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/2024/papers/towards-optimal-convolutional-transfer-learning-architectures-fo.pdf)
- Medical fine-tuning strategy comparison (arXiv 2406.10050): gradual-unfreeze and linear-probe-then-fine-tune (LP-FT) give ~5% accuracy gains over naive fine-tuning; Auto-RGN gives up to ~10% on DenseNet-121 but hurt ResNet-50/VGG-19 (−8–12%) on a tiny 400-image set — strategy choice is architecture- and size-dependent. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10050v1)
- Brain-MRI transfer study (YOLOv10-X on Br35h): freezing the first 5 backbone layers was the empirically best freeze point; freezing 10 layers hurt MRI-domain adaptation, freezing 0 overfit — consistent with a "freeze early, tune late" sweet spot. — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12764844)
- Mixed precision (PyTorch AMP): FP16 matmul peak is 16x FP32 peak on A100; AMP halves activation/weight memory for converted ops, gives 2–3x speedup on Tensor-Core GPUs per the official recipe (up to 3x overall per NVIDIA's guide; 46% faster / ~50% less memory in an AMD demo workload; >30% time and >25% memory savings in a Lightning fine-tuning demo). Use torch.autocast + GradScaler; bf16 preferred if FP16 overflows. — [PyTorch AMP blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/what-every-user-should-know-about-mixed-precision-training-in-pytorch/); [AMP recipe](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/recipes/recipes/amp%5Frecipe.html); [NVIDIA mixed-precision guide (PDF)](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/performance/pdf/Training-Mixed-Precision-User-Guide.pdf)
- Gradient/activation checkpointing: reduces activation memory from O(n) to O(sqrt(n)) layers by recomputing forward activations in backward; canonical result fits >10x larger feed-forward nets at ~20% extra compute; a 1000-layer ResNet drops from ~48 GB to ~7 GB activation memory. Overhead is pure recompute (~20–30% slower steps), no extra CPU-GPU traffic. Pairs well with AMP. — [Lyceum technical guide](https://lyceum.technology/magazine/gradient-checkpointing-memory-savings); [Gradient-checkpointing repo (Salimans/Bulatov)](https://github.com/cybertronai/gradient-checkpointing); [MachineLearningMastery AMP+checkpointing walkthrough](https://machinelearningmastery.com/training-a-model-with-limited-memory-using-mixed-precision-and-gradient-checkpointing)

### Inferences
- Staged recipe for ~4,349 studies on one 12–24 GB GPU: (1) cache frozen-backbone features once, train linear/attention-MIL head in minutes; (2) LoRA/BitFit or top-k-block unfreeze at 384px under AMP; (3) brief full-unfreeze at LR ~1e-4. Step (1) alone should recover the large majority of final AUC per the DINOv2-radiology retention ratios.
- Cached-feature + head-only training cost is UNVERIFIED in GPU-hours for this workload (no citable measurement found), but it removes the backbone backward pass entirely, so per-epoch cost scales with head size only — expected order-of-magnitude cheaper than full fine-tuning; exact factor must be measured in-house.
- Gradient checkpointing + AMP combined should let a 22–89M-param backbone (EfficientNetV2-S through ConvNeXt-B) train at 384px 2.5D on 12 GB where it otherwise OOMs, at roughly 1.2–1.5x step time — UNVERIFIED estimate extrapolated from the cited 20–30% checkpointing overhead and 2–3x AMP speedup ranges.

### Gaps
- No study found reporting "% of full-tune AUC retained by frozen-head-only" specifically on knee MRI or on 12-label macro-AUC; the 96–99% figures are from chest X-ray single/multi-label tasks.
- No measured GPU-hour numbers for cached-feature vs full training on a 4k-study 2.5D knee workload; all cost figures here are relative speedups from other domains.
- Exact freeze-depth (how many early blocks) for ConvNeXt/EfficientNetV2/MaxViT on knee MRI is unreported; the "5 layers" optima come from ResNet/YOLO-style backbones.

## DINOv2 / RadImageNet / domain pretraining for knee MRI: measured gains vs ImageNet pretraining?

### Takeaway
RadImageNet pretraining has the only directly measured knee-MRI (MRNet) gains over ImageNet (+4.8% ACL, +4.5% meniscus AUC, averaged over 24 tuning scenarios), but two independent replications reverse or erase the advantage under different hyperparameters — so the gain is real but protocol-sensitive; DINOv2 is competitive-with-supervised on radiology classification and stronger on segmentation, with PEFT matching full tuning at <1% of params.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv2 pretraining (Meta; TMLR 2024 — venue confirmed as Transactions on Machine Learning Research, NOT a conference): self-supervised on 142M curated images (LVD-142M); ViT-g 1.1B distilled to ViT-S 21M / ViT-B 86M / ViT-L 300M; linear-probe 86.5% / kNN 83.5% on ImageNet-1K, beating OpenCLIP on most image+pixel benchmarks; training itself is 2x faster and 3x less memory than comparable discriminative SSL. Weights/code open. — [TMLR record](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2024/oquab2024tmlr-dinov2/); [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193v2); [Official repo + model table](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2/)
- DINOv2 on radiology (Baharoon et al., >100 experiments, X-ray/CT/MRI): linear-probe DINOv2 beats self-supervised baselines (MAE, MSN) on classification and is competitive with supervised ImageNet-21K ViT and weakly-supervised CLIP/OpenCLIP; on NIH Chest X-ray linear probe ViT-L 0.763 / ViT-g 0.759 vs supervised ViT-L/16-21K 0.751 and DenseNet201 0.735; on brain-tumor MRI 0.962–0.976 across DINOv2 sizes vs 0.933–0.975 supervised; on 3D CT (SARS-CoV-2) 0.922–0.978; kNN underperforms (domain shift), linear-probe and fine-tune do not. Segmentation: frozen DINOv2 beats supervised ViT-L/16-21K on 4/5 organ tasks and beats SAM-encoder features badly on classification (0.755 vs 0.714 NIH). — [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v3)
- RadImageNet (Mei et al., Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, RSNA, 2022; 1.35M CT/MRI/US images, 131,872 patients, 165 labels; weights for ResNet50/DenseNet121/InceptionV3/Inception-ResNet-v2 on GitHub): averaged over 24 LR×freeze scenarios, RadImageNet beats ImageNet by +9.4% thyroid US (0.85 vs 0.76), +4.0% breast US (0.94 vs 0.90), +4.8% ACL MRI (0.97 vs 0.91), +4.5% meniscus MRI (0.96 vs 0.92), all p<.001; large-set gains +6.1% COVID-CT / +1.9% pneumonia / +1.7% SARS-CoV-2 / +0.9% hemorrhage; Grad-CAM Dice +64.6% thyroid / +16.4% breast. Knee sets: 1,021 ACL-tear and 4,201 meniscus images from MRNet. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9530758); [Weights repo](https://github.com/BMEII-AI/RadImageNet); [HuggingFace mirror](https://huggingface.co/Lab-Rasool/RadImageNet)
- Contradiction — replication (Dei et al., arXiv 2302.08272, CCA study on 7 targets incl. knee): ResNet50 ImageNet init beats RadImageNet init on 6/7 datasets; knee is the one exception where RadImageNet wins but by a margin the authors call comparable; ImageNet weights also change less during fine-tuning; transfer ranking is hyperparameter-sensitive, not an inherent property of the source. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08272v3)
- Contradiction — Stanford CS231N 2024 (ResNet50/InceptionV3/DenseNet121, tuned per-task): best ImageNet-init models beat best RadImageNet-init models with DeLong significance on both tasks — ACL 0.9969 vs 0.9594, breast 0.9641 vs 0.8889; RadImageNet helped only early-epoch ACL convergence before plateauing; authors attribute ImageNet's win partly to RadImageNet's TF-vs-PyTorch weight conversions and missing breast-US content in RadImageNet. — [CS231N paper PDF](https://cs231n.stanford.edu/2024/papers/towards-optimal-convolutional-transfer-learning-architectures-fo.pdf)
- Contradiction — dental replication (Okazaki et al., J. Digital Imaging/Springer 2024/2025): RadImageNet avg AUC 0.68 vs ImageNet 0.74 on panoramic radiographs (Δ −6.5%, p<.01 favoring ImageNet) and 0.76 vs 0.75 on cephalometric (Δ +0.7%, n.s.) — domain pretraining does not transfer to unseen modalities/anatomy. Consistent with lung-US and PET/CT reports where ImageNet won. — [Springer article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-024-01204-9)
- Editorial context (Cadrin-Chênevert, Radiology: AI 2022): RadImageNet contains no plain radiographs, yet still beat ImageNet on a chest-X-ray task — attributed to grayscale-pretrained low-level filters; recommends future versions add radiography/PET and more classes. — [RSNA editorial](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/ryai.220126)

### Inferences
- For knee MRI specifically, RadImageNet init is the best-evidenced domain-pretraining bet (it contains knee MRI and the MRNet-derived gains are the only knee-specific head-to-head numbers), but the two reversals mean the protocol must fix architecture + LR schedule + freeze depth per init rather than reusing one grid for both.
- DINOv2 ViT-B (86M) or ViT-S (21M) with frozen/PEFT tuning is the most GPU-friendly foundation-model path; ViT-g (1.1B) cannot be fully fine-tuned on 12–24 GB at 384px — linear/LoRA/BitFit only. DINOv2's MRI evidence is brain-tumor type classification, not knee — knee transfer is UNVERIFIED.
- Practical hedge supported by evidence: train one RadImageNet-init CNN arm and one ImageNet- or DINOv2-init transformer arm and ensemble them (next section), since the CCA replication shows the two inits converge to distinct representations yet similar predictions — exactly the diversity ensembles exploit.

### Gaps
- No DINOv2-vs-ImageNet head-to-head on knee MRI or on 12-label knee macro-AUC found; no DINOv3 existence/numbers verified.
- RadImageNet ships only ResNet50/DenseNet121/InceptionV3/Inception-ResNet-v2 weights — no ConvNeXt, EfficientNetV2, MaxViT, or ViT variants — so modern-backbone + RadImageNet-pretraining combos are unavailable off the shelf.
- The RSNA-knee-2026 Kaggle forum was suggested as a source but no accessible citable backbone-comparison numbers were retrieved.

## CNN+transformer backbone mixes in one ensemble: measured decorrelation/gain?

### Takeaway
Mixing CNN and transformer backbones in one ensemble is consistently reported as the best-performing combination, with the single cleanest medical measurement (ACCV 2022 workshop: CSwin-Transformer + CNN ensemble beats the best single transformer and human experts), but no study found quantifies prediction decorrelation (correlation/Q-statistic) on knee MRI.

### Cited Findings
- Biliary-atresia medical ensemble (Wei et al., ACCV 2022 workshop, CVMC): best single model CSwin Transformer 87.86% accuracy (precision 88.07%, recall 87.74%, AUC 90.78%); ViT-family singles beat CNN singles; every ensemble beats every single and beats the human expert; the ViT+CNN cross-family ensemble is the overall winner on both clean and noisy (external) data. — [ACCV workshop paper (PDF)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2022W/CVMC/papers/Wei_Ensemble_Model_of_Visual_Transformer_and_CNN_Helps_BA_Diagnosis_ACCVW_2022_paper.pdf)
- TransUNet (WACV 2022): the hybrid CNN-encoder + Transformer bottleneck beats pure-CNN and pure-Transformer segmentation baselines, establishing the "CNN local texture + Transformer global context are complementary, not competing" design pattern reused by MaxViT-UNet and successors. — [TransUNet retrospective (MedGemma)](https://medgemma.pro/blog/transformers-medical-image-segmentation)
- Skin-lesion diagnosis (Erbay et al., ScienceDirect 2025): ensemble of CNN and transformer architectures improves multi-class diagnosis over either family alone. — [ScienceDirect article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2215098625002009)
- Brain-MRI transformer ensemble (Latif et al., Scientific Reports/Nature 2025): deep ensemble of transformer models for brain MRI analysis. — [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-08362-y)
- Representation-diversity mechanism (Dei et al. CCA replication, arXiv 2302.08272): ImageNet-init and RadImageNet-init ResNet50s converge to distinct intermediate representations that diverge further during fine-tuning (similarity near random-init levels in late layers) while making similar predictions — i.e., different pretraining sources learn genuinely different features for the same task, the precondition for ensemble gains. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08272v3)
- Cross-task generality (Baharoon et al. DINOv2 radiology study): DINOv2 ranks #1 on average for classification while MAE (masked-image-modeling SSL) ranks high only on segmentation and CLIP-style weakly-supervised models only on classification — a model that is best at one task family is not best at the other, so a CNN+Transformer ensemble pools complementary error modes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02366v3)

### Inferences
- Recommended ensemble-decorrelation lever for the 12-label knee task: pair a RadImageNet- or ImageNet-init CNN (ConvNeXt-T/S or EfficientNetV2-S) with a transformer/hybrid (MaxViT-S/T or DINOv2 ViT-B with PEFT) under the same 2.5D-window MIL head, then average probabilities; add a third arm only if validation shows residual complementary errors.
- Expected ensemble gain magnitude is UNVERIFIED for knee macro-AUC (no citable number); by analogy to the ACCV result (ensemble > best single > human expert) a small but reliable +0.5–1.5% macro-AUC over the best single arm is a plausible planning assumption — must be measured, not assumed.

### Gaps
- No knee-MRI study found reporting pairwise prediction correlations, Q-statistics, or disagreement rates between CNN and transformer arms, nor an ablation of heterogeneous (CNN+Transformer) vs homogeneous (CNN+CNN) ensembles on macro-AUC.
- No RSNA 2026 Kaggle ensemble-writeup numbers retrieved; no measurement of ensemble cost (2–3x training/inference) against the 12–24 GB single-GPU constraint for 384px 2.5D inference.
